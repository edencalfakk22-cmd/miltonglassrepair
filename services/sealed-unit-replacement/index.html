Fog between the panes is a failed seal. A double-glazed unit is two sheets of glass bonded around a spacer with a sealed, usually gas-filled cavity between them, and that cavity is what insulates. Southern Ontario works it hard in both directions — humid summers expanding the trapped air, deep-freeze winters contracting it — and eventually the perimeter seal breaks down, moisture moves in and the gas fill is gone. What you can see is the haze. What you have actually lost is the thermal performance you paid for.

In Milton this arrives in cohorts. Hawthorne Village, Beaty, Clarke, Coates and Harrison filled in over a compressed period, so entire streets are reaching this point within a few years of one another — one hazed pane this spring and two more on the same elevation next spring, all of them obvious at once in low winter sun. The window itself is fine. We measure the unit, record the sight size, thickness, spacer, tint and any low-e coating, and fit a replacement built to that make-up into the sash you already own. The frame stays in the wall and nothing needs repainting afterwards.

FAQs

Sealed Unit (Thermal Pane) Replacement — your questions

The house is only a few years old and a unit has already fogged — is that normal?

It is early, and it is worth pursuing rather than absorbing. An early seal failure is usually a manufacturing or installation issue, and on a newer home it may sit within your Tarion coverage or the window manufacturer's own warranty. Talk to the builder or the window supplier before you pay anyone. We will document what we find in writing if that helps you make the case.

Can you tell whether the argon has actually gone?

Not by looking, and anyone who says otherwise is guessing. What is visible is the seal failure — the haze, streaking or droplets between the panes that will not wipe from either side. Once the seal is breached the fill is not staying in, so in practical terms a fogged unit has lost it. We will not put a performance number on your window that we cannot measure.

Is a patio door unit a bigger job than a window?

Bigger and heavier, yes. A patio door insulating unit is often toughened as well, because it is glass in a door, and it usually takes two people and proper suction gear to handle safely. The principle is identical — the unit comes out of the sash and a new one goes in — but the handling and the lead time are different, and we say which you are dealing with at the measure-up.

Can this be done in the middle of winter?

Yes. The sash stays in the wall and the opening is only exposed for the few minutes the unit is being swapped, so it is not a summer-only job. If anything, autumn and winter are when people notice the problem — a blown unit is coldest and sweats most when the heating is running hardest.
