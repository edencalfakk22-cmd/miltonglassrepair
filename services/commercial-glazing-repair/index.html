Aluminum storefront framing is a system, and glass is only one component of it. The parts that actually fail first are the ones nobody looks at: gaskets that have gone hard and shrunk back from the corners, setting blocks that have flattened or slipped, thresholds packed with salt and grit, drainage that no longer drains, and joints that have opened up as the whole assembly moves through a Milton winter. Once any of that is wrong, water sits where it should not and the next pane you buy cracks for reasons that have nothing to do with the glass.

We repair the framing rather than glazing over it. Across the Derry Green business park, the Louis St. Laurent and Bronte Street South plazas and the office-front units around town, that means renewing gaskets and stops, correcting or replacing setting blocks, clearing and re-forming weeps so the pocket sheds water, making good corroded and impact-damaged sections, and re-sealing joints properly. Where a section is beyond repair we say so rather than patching it, and where it is repairable we say that too — a landlord replacing a whole frame that needed a gasket set has been sold something.

FAQs

Commercial Glazing & Aluminum Frame Repair — your questions

Our frame is corroded at the bottom — repair or replace?

It depends how far it has gone, and that is a judgement made on site rather than from a photograph. Surface corrosion and a pitted finish on aluminum are usually cosmetic and can be treated; structural loss at the base of a mullion, or a section that has lost its ability to hold a setting block, is not repairable and we will say so. What we will not do is quote a full frame replacement for a problem a gasket set would fix.

Water is coming in at the bottom of the frontage but the glass is fine — what is that?

Almost always drainage. Aluminum storefront systems are designed to let a certain amount of water into the glazing pocket and drain it back out through weep holes; when those block with grit, salt or old sealant, the water has nowhere to go but inside. We clear and re-form the weeps, check the setting blocks and re-seal what needs it. Sealing the outside of the frame is usually the wrong fix and traps the water in.

Do the panes have to come out for frame work?

For some of it, yes — you cannot renew a gasket set or correct setting blocks with the glass in place. For threshold work, corrosion treatment, sealing and drainage work, generally not. We tell you which parts of the scope need the glass out when we quote, because that affects the timing and whether the unit can stay open.

Can this be scheduled rather than done reactively?

It should be. Gaskets, weeps, thresholds and door hardware on a commercial frontage are wear items, and looking at them before winter is far cheaper than dealing with what happens after it. We are happy to walk a plaza or a business-park unit, write down what needs attention and what does not, and let you plan it rather than react to it.
