Door glass is glass that gets swung, and that is why it breaks. Hydraulic fluid in a closer thickens as the temperature drops, so an entrance adjusted in July starts driving the door into the frame in January; on a house, a patio door that has dropped on its rollers or a front door that has swollen and now needs a shoulder puts load on the lite every time it is used. Add salt and grit packed into a threshold and the freeze-thaw movement that works a frame loose over a Milton winter, and the pane is the part that eventually gives up.

We look at the whole opening for that reason. Pivots, hinges and rollers, closer speed and latch action, the sweep, the threshold and whether the door and frame are still square — all of it, because a new pane in a door that still slams is a return visit already booked. Aluminum entrances, patio sliders, sidelites, transoms and the decorative inserts that came with a lot of the newer subdivision front doors are all re-glazed in the safety glass the position requires, and the door goes back swinging and latching properly.

FAQs

Door Glass & Entrance Glazing — your questions

The decorative insert in our front door is cracked — can just that be replaced?

Usually, yes. Most decorative and leaded-look inserts on modern doors sit in a frame that unscrews from the door face, so the insert comes out as a cassette and a replacement goes back in without touching the door slab, the hardware or the finish. Matching the pattern is the part that takes time, and we identify it before ordering rather than after.

Our patio slider has cracked and it is also getting harder to move — related?

Very likely. Grit and salt build up in the bottom track, rollers wear unevenly, and the panel starts running out of square. Once that happens the glass carries loads it was never meant to. We clear and check the track and rollers as part of the re-glaze, because otherwise the new unit inherits exactly the same problem.

Is the door glass covered by our new-home warranty?

It may be, and it is worth asking before you pay for it. Tarion coverage on a new home can include defects and damage identified within the applicable period, so on a recently built house the first call is the builder. We will attend, document what we find and quote it, which is generally what the builder wants to see anyway.

Can you sort the closer or the swing while you are there?

That is the part that makes the new glass last. Setting the closing speed and latch action for winter rather than for the day we happen to be there, checking pivots and the sweep, and clearing the threshold are all included in how we approach the job. If a closer or a roller set is genuinely worn out rather than out of adjustment, we say so plainly.
