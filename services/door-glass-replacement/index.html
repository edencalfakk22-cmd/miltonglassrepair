Door glass in Milton usually breaks for a reason that has nothing to do with the glass. A hydraulic closer thickens as the temperature drops, the door starts slamming through January, and the pane takes that impact several hundred times a day. Meanwhile salt and grit ground into the threshold wear the bottom pivot, the door drops out of square, and the glass ends up carrying load it was never meant to. So we re-glaze and we look at the whole assembly — frame, stops, gaskets, pivots and the swing itself — so the new pane is not simply the next one to break.

Entrance doors, sidelites and transoms all get tempered or laminated safety glazing, which is what the Ontario Building Code expects at an entrance. Fire-rated doors are a separate matter and we treat them that way: a vision panel in a labelled fire door has to be labelled fire-protection-rated glazing with its certification stamp intact, and the Ontario Fire Code requires those door assemblies — glazing, self-closing device and latch together — to be maintained in working order. Ordinary glass never goes into a labelled fire door, no matter what someone put in there last time. Most Milton doors can be re-glazed in place without coming off their hinges or pivots.

FAQs

Door Glass & Entrance Glazing — your questions

The glass in my aluminum entrance door keeps breaking — why?

Nine times out of ten the door is the culprit, not the glass. A closer that is out of adjustment or has thickened in the cold slams the door, or a worn pivot has let the door drop out of square so the frame is loading the pane. We check the swing, the pivots and the closer when we re-glaze, so the replacement is not on the same countdown.

Does door glass have to be safety glass?

Yes. Glass in a door, in a sidelite wide enough to be mistaken for a doorway, and in entrance panels has to be tempered or laminated safety glass to CAN/CGSB-12.1-M. We replace it with the correct type rather than plain glass, and we confirm which one your opening needs before anything is cut.

The vision panel in our fire door is cracked — can you just put regular glass in it?

No, and nobody should. A labelled fire door needs labelled fire-protection-rated glazing sized and installed for that assembly, with the stamp left visible in the corner. The Ontario Fire Code requires the closure to be maintained so it self-closes and latches, so we check the closer and latch at the same time and tell you if the door itself is the reason the glazing failed.
