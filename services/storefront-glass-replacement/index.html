FAQs

Storefront Glass Replacement — your questions

Our Main Street unit has no rear access — how does a large pane even get in?

Carefully, and it is worked out before the day rather than on it. Access, the width of the sidewalk, where the truck can sit and whether the lite can be turned inside the unit all get looked at during the measure-up, and the crew and gear are sized to that. It is the reason we would rather quote a downtown frontage after seeing it than off a photograph.

The frame is visibly out of square — will a new pane even fit it?

It will if it is cut to the opening rather than to nominal dimensions. A frame that has settled over a century is not rectangular, and glass ordered to the drawing binds in one corner and rattles in the other. We take the actual sightline and rebate, allow for what the frame is doing, and specify to that. Where the frame has moved far enough that it will not hold glass safely, we tell you before ordering.

How long will we be closed while the work is done?

Rarely a full day. If the opening is standing open we board it first so you can trade in the meantime, and the glazing itself is scheduled around your hours where the access allows. A large lite needs the area in front of it clear for a while, but that is usually a couple of hours rather than a closure.

A contractor chipped the edge of our new storefront glass — does that matter?

It matters more than a chip in the middle of a pane. The edge is where stress concentrates and where a crack will start, so an edge chip on a large lite is effectively a scheduled failure. If it happened during construction it is worth raising with the contractor before you replace it, and we are happy to put in writing what we found and why it needs doing.

How much does storefront glass replacement cost in Milton?

A Milton storefront is quoted per opening once measured. The size, thickness and whether the position needs laminated rather than toughened glass set the baseline; then it is how much the frame needs before it will hold glass again, how the pane has to be brought to the opening, and whether a board-up came first. Heritage frontages that have to be matched take longer. The assessment is free.
