In Ontario, safety glazing is a matter of where the glass sits. Doors, sidelites wide enough to be mistaken for a doorway, panes low enough to walk into, guards, and shower and tub enclosures all have to meet CAN/CGSB-12.1-M — the standard the Building Code points at. Tempered glass meets it by heat treatment and breaks into small blunt pellets. Laminated meets it by bonding two panes to a plastic interlayer, so it cracks but stays in the opening, which is what you want anywhere falling glass would land on somebody.

Beyond the code positions there is a lot of made-to-size work that simply ought to be toughened: shelving carrying stock, tabletops people lean on, glazed partitions, and the odd shapes that come out of a custom build. All of it is fabricated to a measurement we take, because tempered glass cannot be cut, drilled or edge-worked once it has been through the furnace — every dimension, hole, notch and polished edge has to be specified before toughening. That is why we measure openings ourselves rather than working from figures over the phone, and why we ask about hardware positions before anything is ordered.

FAQs

Tempered & Safety Glass Cut to Size — your questions

Does the glass in my side garage door or basement walk-out need to be safety glass?

Glass in a door does, without exception — a side entry door, a walk-out, a garage man-door, all of them. So does a sidelite beside it that is wide enough to be mistaken for a doorway, and low glazing anyone could walk into. We check the specific opening rather than answering by category, but the safe expectation on any door is that the answer is yes.

We want a glass tabletop and some shelving — what should we ask for?

Toughened, with polished edges, made to a template. Annealed glass at those thicknesses breaks into large blades and the raw edges are a hazard in their own right before anything breaks. Tell us the thickness you want it to feel like, what it has to carry and whether the corners need radiusing, and we will specify it and have it made to your dimensions.

Can the edges be finished so they are safe to handle?

Yes, and on anything free-standing they should be. Arrised, ground, flat-polished and pencil-polished are different finishes with different looks, and they all have to be done before the glass is toughened. It is worth deciding the finish at the same time as the size, because it cannot be added afterwards.

How do you get a large made-to-size lite into a house out in the new subdivisions?

Planned, rather than improvised. Access, stair turns, doorway widths and whether the panel can be stood up inside the room all get looked at when we measure, and the crew and gear are matched to it. A panel that fits the opening but cannot be carried to it is a wasted fabrication, so it is a question we ask early.
